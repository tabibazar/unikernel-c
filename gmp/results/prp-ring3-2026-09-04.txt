what this overturns
-------------------
docs/portfolio/CUNNINGHAM-SIZE-RECORDS.md carried a 3.7x BareMetal speedup and
applied it to the chain-hunt cost. That 3.7x was measured in
docs/nanos-vs-baremetal/ on 64-bit modular arithmetic -- small, register-resident
work where syscall and scheduler overhead dominate and removing the OS helps.

It does not transfer. On GMP big-integer arithmetic the two are the same speed
to within 0.2%. At 3366 bits the work is memory- and ALU-bound inside libgmp
with essentially no OS interaction per unit of work, so there is no OS overhead
left to remove. The absence of an operating system buys nothing here.

The chain-hunt core-hour figure should be taken from the measured 21,326, with
no unikernel discount applied.
