The problem

Two questions a trading desk asks every day.

What is this option worth? — and how much could I lose?

Real portfolios have no pricing formula, and risk is not one number but a whole loss distribution. The only general tool is simulation: draw thousands of random futures, price each, and read the answer off the spread.

A quant trick, shown honestly

4×

less variance, for free.

Pair every random shock Z with its mirror −Z — antithetic variates. The two cancel each other's noise, so the error bar halves without a single extra sample.

plain 10.4486 +- 0.0052 antithetic 10.4521 +- 0.0026 (4.0x)

Why a unikernel, and not a container

Every run is logged, and every run is reproducible.

Each simulation writes an immutable record to Redis — the audit trail MiFID II and SEC rules require. And reproducibility is not free: this same platform silently computed wrong 1 in 4,000 times until we root-caused and fixed it.

A risk number you cannot reproduce is a risk number you cannot defend.

Determinism is the product. We found the one bug that broke it, and closed it.

7 / 9

Where the randomness came from

The CPU's own entropy, proven clean.

Millions of Gaussian shocks, each built from RDRAND — the hardware source shown the day before to pass ent, FIPS 140-2 and dieharder. A unikernel has no /dev/random; this one makes its own, correctly.

No math library either: exp, ln, sqrt and the normal distribution are a few dozen lines of C. The program is the machine.
