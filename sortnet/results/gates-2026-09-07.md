# Gates G1 and G2

Per `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-05-sorting-network-gp-design.md`. The gates
are exact published optima, so they are pass/fail rather than judgement, and the
statistic is the **median of 10 seeds** — not the best, and failed seeds are
recorded at the full budget rather than dropped.

## Evaluator, before any search existed

```
n=4, optimal 5 comparators             16 / 16    ok
n=8, optimal 19 comparators           256 / 256   ok
n=4, 4 comparators (broken)            12 / 16    ok
n=4, empty network                      5 / 16    ok
n=6, empty network                      7 / 64    ok
n=4 empty: already-sorted count         5 / 5     ok
SELFTEST PASSED
```

Three of these matter more than the two obvious ones. The **broken** network
must score imperfectly, or a "sorted-unconditionally" evaluator would pass a
suite of only-correct networks. The **empty** network must score exactly n+1 —
the monotone 0-1 vectors — which catches an evaluator that miscounts rather than
one that merely fails. And the n=8 network scoring a perfect 256 cross-confirms
the published optimum of 19.

## Results

| gate | n | target | seeds solved | median evaluations | budget | |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| G1 | 4 | 5 comparators | 10/10 | **253** | 100,000 | PASS |
| G2 | 6 | 12 comparators | 10/10 | **3,692** | 1,000,000 | PASS |

G2 spread: 1,767 / 2,328 / 2,648 / 2,707 / 3,180 / 4,205 / 4,389 / 5,807 /
14,056 / 26,466. A 15× spread across seeds is normal for this kind of search and
is exactly why the gate is a median rather than a best.

Both clear their budgets by two to three orders of magnitude, so the budgets are
not the binding constraint at these sizes.

## Two bugs the first run exposed

**The seeds were not independent.** The RNG was splitmix64 with
`state = seed * GAMMA`, where GAMMA is also splitmix's increment — so every seed
lands on the same arithmetic progression. Verified directly: seed 2's stream is
seed 1's shifted by exactly one draw, seed 3's by two.

```
seed 1: 346edce5f713f8ed 1e9a57bc80e6721d 2d160e7e5c3f42ca ...
seed 2: 1e9a57bc80e6721d 2d160e7e5c3f42ca 81c2e6dc980d78eb ...
seed 3: 2d160e7e5c3f42ca 81c2e6dc980d78eb 5647e55ad933f62e ...
```

Ten "independent" seeds were one sequence sampled at ten offsets, and the
duplicate results gave it away — seeds 1 and 2 both solving at 600, seeds 8 and
9 both at 422, three G2 seeds all at 6,836. **A median over ten copies of the
same run is not a median**, which defeats the entire point of the gate. The RNG
is now xoshiro256** with splitmix64 used only as a seeder, which is its
documented role.

**Solutions found in the initial population reported `evals=0`.** Five of ten G1
seeds showed it. Building the population costs 200 evaluations, so zero was both
impossible and flattering. Now reported as the true count — which is why two G1
seeds legitimately read exactly 200.

Neither bug would have failed a gate. Both would have made the gates mean less
than they appear to, which is worse: a gate that cannot fail is not a gate.
